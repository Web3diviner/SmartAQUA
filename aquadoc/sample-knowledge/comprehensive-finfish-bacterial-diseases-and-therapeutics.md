## 1. Motile Aeromonas Septicemia (MAS), Ulcer Disease & Hemorrhagic Septicemia
- **Causative Agents:** *Aeromonas hydrophila*, *Aeromonas caviae*, *Aeromonas sobria*, *Aeromonas veronii* (Gram-negative motile rods, facultative anaerobes).
- **Target Species:** African catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*), Tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*), Common Carp, Pangasius, Striped Bass.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Elevated water temperature (25–32°C), high un-ionized ammonia ($NH_3 > 0.05\text{ mg/L}$), poor organic sediment hygiene, rough handling during netting/sorting, and parasitic skin damage.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - External: Raised red hemorrhagic blotches on flanks, deep necrotic skin ulcers exposing musculature, ragged fin rot, protruding anus/vent, bilateral exophthalmia (popeye), and abdominal distension (dropsy/ascites).
  - Internal / Necropsy: Straw-colored or bloody peritoneal ascitic fluid, enlarged friable pale or congested liver, swollen black spleen, and severely inflamed petechial intestines.
- **Disease Course & Mortality:** Acute outbreaks cause 10%–25% daily mortality if untreated; subacute/chronic forms cause lingering 1%–3% daily loss and emaciation.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Immediate First Aid:** Withhold feed for 24–48 hours; perform 40%–50% water exchange with clean, well-aerated borehole water; increase aeration (DO > 5.5 mg/L).
  - **Osmoregulatory Support Bath:** Non-iodized crude salt (NaCl) at 3 to 5 ppt (3–5 kg per 1,000 L) continuous tank bath.
  - **Botanical Antimicrobial Therapy:** Fresh aqueous Bitter Leaf (*Vernonia amygdalina*) extract bath at 50–100 mL per 100 L water for 24–48 hours; in-feed dried bitter leaf meal at 15–25 g/kg feed (1.5%–2.5%) combined with fresh crushed garlic paste at 10–20 g/kg feed (1%–2%) for 10–14 days.
  - **Veterinary Prescriptive Antibiotic Regimens:**
    * *Oxytetracycline HCl:* 50–75 mg per kg fish biomass per day in medicated feed for 7–10 consecutive days. (Mandatory 21-day withdrawal period before food harvest).
    * *Florfenicol:* 10 mg per kg fish biomass per day in medicated feed for 10 consecutive days. (15-day withdrawal period).

## 2. Columnaris Disease, Saddleback & Cotton Mouth Disease
- **Causative Agent:** *Flavobacterium columnare* (Gram-negative long slender gliding filamentous rod).
- **Target Species:** African catfish, Tilapia, Channel catfish, Carp, Aquarium finfish.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Water temperatures > 26°C, high organic loading, crowded stocking density, mechanical abrasions from rough grading or transport.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - "Saddleback" lesion: White, yellowish, or gray discoloration and band of tissue necrosis surrounding the dorsal fin.
  - Cotton mouth: Yellowish-white necrotic plaques on lips, mouth, and barbels.
  - Gill lesions: Rapid necrosis starting from gill tips inward, producing yellowish mucus mats and severe respiratory distress.
  - High lethargy, fish hanging vertically at the water surface or resting listlessly near inflows.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Water Sanitation:** Maintain low water temperature if controllable; flush organic bottom waste; maintain DO > 6.0 mg/L.
  - **Salt Therapy:** Continuous immersion bath in non-iodized salt (NaCl) at 3 to 5 ppt for 5–7 days; or rapid dip at 15 ppt (15 g/L) for 5–10 minutes under constant aeration.
  - **Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$) Bath:** 2–4 mg/L (ppm) static bath for 30–45 minutes with strong aeration (water should change from wine-purple to brown).
  - **Antimicrobial Chemotherapy:** *Oxytetracycline* in-feed at 55–75 mg/kg biomass/day for 10 days, or *Sulfadimethoxine-Ormetoprim* (Romet-30) at 50 mg/kg biomass/day for 5 days.

## 3. Edwardsiellosis & Emphysematous Putrefactive Disease of Catfish (EPDC)
- **Causative Agents:** *Edwardsiella ictaluri* (Enteric Septicemia of Catfish / ESC) and *Edwardsiella tarda* (EPDC / Fish Gangrene).
- **Target Species:** *Clarias gariepinus*, *Heterobranchus*, Channel catfish, Tilapia, Eel.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Water temperature fluctuations between 22°C and 28°C, overfeeding, decomposing organic sludge, high nitrite and ammonia.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - *Edwardsiella ictaluri (ESC / "Hole-in-the-Head"):*
    * Petechial hemorrhages around jaws, mouth, and belly.
    * Characteristic "Hole-in-the-head" skull erosion directly between the eyes along the skull sutures.
    * Erratic spiral swimming ("corkscrewing"), hanging vertically with head at water surface.
  - *Edwardsiella tarda (EPDC / Gangrene):*
    * Deep muscle abscesses that rupture and emit a foul, putrid gas odor (hydrogen sulfide/methane smell).
    * Necrotic liquefactive lesions in trunk muscles, loss of skin pigmentation, and open muscle fistulae.
    * Internal: Pale liver with discrete white necrotizing granulomas, swollen spleen, and renomegaly.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Immediate Ration Reduction:** Immediately stop feed or cut ration by 75% for 48 hours.
  - **Systemic Antibiotic Chemotherapy:**
    * *Florfenicol:* 10 mg/kg biomass/day orally in feed for 10 days.
    * *Oxytetracycline:* 75 mg/kg biomass/day for 10 days.
  - **Immune Enhancement:** Top-dress feed with Coated Vitamin C (Stay-C) at 1,000 mg/kg feed and probiotic *Bacillus subtilis* ($1\times 10^9\text{ CFU/kg}$) for 21 days.

## 4. Streptococcosis & Lactococcosis (Warmwater Finfish Meningitis)
- **Causative Agents:** *Streptococcus iniae*, *Streptococcus agalactiae* (Lancefield Group B), *Lactococcus garvieae* (Gram-positive cocci in chains/pairs).
- **Target Species:** Tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*), Barramundi, Rainbow Trout, Catfish, Sea Bass.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Water temperatures > 28°C–32°C, high stocking densities in cages or intensive RAS tanks, low DO (< 3.0 mg/L), high salinity fluctuations.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Neurological signs: "C-shaped" curved spinal posture, erratic hyperactive swimming, whirling, spiraling along bottom.
  - Ocular pathology: Severe bilateral exophthalmia ("Popeye") with corneal opacity, hyphema (blood in anterior chamber of eye), or complete panophthalmitis.
  - External/Internal: Bloody hemorrhages on operculum and fin bases; purulent exudate in pericardium and brain meninges; enlarged black spleen.
  - Acute mortality in market-size adult tilapia (up to 30%–70% mortality in untreated cages).
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Biosecurity & Density Management:** Thin out stock density; reduce feeding rate; maximize water turnover and emergency liquid oxygenation.
  - **Veterinary Antibiotic Chemotherapy (Gram-Positive Targeted):**
    * *Florfenicol:* 10–15 mg/kg fish body weight/day in feed for 10 consecutive days.
    * *Amoxicillin Trihydrate:* 40–80 mg/kg fish biomass/day orally in feed for 7–10 days. (15-day withdrawal).
    * *Erythromycin:* 50–100 mg/kg biomass/day for 10 days.
  - **Vaccination / Prevention:** Polyvalent formalin-inactivated bivalent *S. iniae / S. agalactiae* vaccines administered via intraperitoneal injection or immersion to fingerlings > 15g.

## 5. Vibriosis (Red Boil Disease / Saltwater & Brackish Septicemia)
- **Causative Agents:** *Vibrio anguillarum*, *Vibrio vulnificus*, *Vibrio parahaemolyticus*, *Photobacterium damselae* (Gram-negative curved halophilic rods).
- **Target Species:** Asian Sea Bass, Tilapia in brackish water, Salmonids, Grouper, Shrimps.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Salinities > 5–35 ppt, warm temperatures > 24°C, skin abrasions, hypoxic stress.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Severe dark skin discoloration, large bloody boil-like lesions (furuncles) on the flanks that rupture discharging red bloody exudate.
  - Extensive bleeding at the base of pectoral and anal fins; severe anemia with pale gills; bloody ascites in the gut.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - Rapid freshwater bath dip (lowering salinity to 0–2 ppt for 10–15 minutes) to disrupt bacterial halophilic membrane balance.
  - Oral *Oxytetracycline* at 75 mg/kg/day or *Florfenicol* at 10 mg/kg/day for 10 days.

## 6. Bacterial Gill Disease (BGD)
- **Causative Agent:** *Flavobacterium branchiophilum* (Gram-negative filamentous bacterium).
- **Target Species:** Salmonid fry, Catfish fingerlings, Carp, Tilapia nurseries.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** High suspended solids / silt turbidity, high ammonia levels, overcrowded hatchery troughs.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Fish gather at inflow with flared opercula, gasping for air; gills appear swollen, pale, and covered with copious thick mucus.
  - Microscopically: Clubbing and fusion of gill lamellae with masses of filamentous bacteria.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - Clean filters, remove suspended debris, and improve flow rate.
  - **Chloramine-T Bath:** 8.5–12 mg/L static bath for 60 minutes with continuous aeration for 2–3 alternate days.
  - **Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$) Bath:** 100–250 mg/L for 30 minutes under rigorous dissolved oxygen monitoring.

## 7. Bacterial Kidney Disease (BKD)
- **Causative Agent:** *Renibacterium salmoninarum* (Gram-positive small intracellular diplobacillus).
- **Target Species:** Salmonids (Trout, Salmon), occasionally coolwater finfish.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Chronic granulomatous disease: Swollen gray-white nodular lesions throughout the posterior kidney and spleen.
  - Exophthalmia, abdominal distension, petechial hemorrhages at the base of fins.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - Intracellular nature makes total cure difficult; culling and strict broodstock screening via ELISA/PCR.
  - In-feed *Erythromycin* (100 mg/kg/day for 21 days) or *Azithromycin* under strict veterinary supervision.

## 8. Enteric Redmouth Disease (ERM / Yersiniosis)
- **Causative Agent:** *Yersinia ruckeri* (Gram-negative non-motile rod).
- **Target Species:** Rainbow trout, Atlantic salmon, occasionally catfish and carp.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Characteristic intense subcutaneous hemorrhaging in and around the mouth ("Redmouth"), throat, tongue, and opercular covers.
  - Exophthalmia with ring hemorrhages around the iris; gut filled with yellowish opaque fluid.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - Oral *Oxytetracycline* (75 mg/kg/day for 10 days) or *Tribrissen* (Sulfadiazine-Trimethoprim at 30 mg/kg/day for 7–10 days).
  - Commercial immersion vaccination of fry at 5g size provides robust lifetime protection.

## 9. Mycobacteriosis (Fish Tuberculosis / Piscine Granuloma) — ZOONOTIC WARNING
- **Causative Agents:** *Mycobacterium marinum*, *Mycobacterium fortuitum*, *Mycobacterium chelonae* (Acid-fast pleomorphic bacilli).
- **Target Species:** All finfish species (Catfish, Tilapia, Seabass, Aquarium fish).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Chronic wasting disease: Hollow belly, emaciation, bent spine (scoliosis/lordosis), scale loss, non-healing skin granulomas.
  - Internal: Countless tiny gray-white "miliary" granulomatous nodules studded across liver, spleen, and kidney (acid-fast positive on Ziehl-Neelsen staining).
- **Zoonotic Threat:** Can infect human skin causing "Swimming Pool Granuloma" or "Fish Handler's Disease" (nodular ulcerating skin lesions on hands and forearms). Handlers must wear protective rubber gloves.
- **Clinical Action & Control:**
  - Incurable with standard fish antibiotics. Infected commercial stocks must be humanely culled and ponds drained, disinfected with 200 ppm chlorine or 2% sodium hydroxide ($NaOH$).
