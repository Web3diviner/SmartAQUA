## Section 1: Commercial Floating Feeds vs. Sinking Farm-Made Pellets in Nigeria

In Nigerian commercial catfish farming, feed accounts for **65% to 75% of total production operational costs**. Farmers utilize two primary feeding regimes:

### 1. Commercial Extruded Floating Feeds
- **Brands in West Africa:** Skretting, Aller Aqua, Coppens, Durante, Blue Crown, TopFeed, Aqualis, EcoFloat.
- **Advantages:**
  - High water stability (> 20 minutes floating) allows 100% visual inspection of fish appetite and prevents overfeeding.
  - High digestibility and energy density; typical Crude Protein: **42% to 56%**.
  - Consistent Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR): **0.95 to 1.15** under good water quality.
- **Application:** Essential during the nursery, fingerling, and juvenile stages (up to 200g–300g).

### 2. On-Farm Extruded or Sinking Pellets (Grow-Out Phase)
- Formulated using locally available agro-industrial byproducts to reduce operational costs from 300g to table size (1kg+).
- **Key Local Ingredients:**
  - **Animal Protein Sources:** Local fishmeal (Ogbomoso / Lagos marine waste 60–65% CP), imported 72% Danish fishmeal, blood meal (80–85% CP), poultry byproduct meal.
  - **Plant Protein Sources:** Toasted full-fat soybean meal (44–48% CP), Groundnut Cake (GNC - 42–46% CP), palm kernel cake (PKC - limited to < 8% due to high fiber).
  - **Energy & Binders:** Yellow maize meal, wheat offal, cassava flour (gelatinized starch binder 8–12%), palm oil / crude fish oil (energy and attractant 2–4%).
  - **Essential Additives:** Dicalcium Phosphate (DCP 1.5%), Lysine (0.3%), Methionine (0.3%), Vitamin & Mineral Premix (0.5%), Coated Vitamin C (0.1%), Toxin Binder (0.2%).

## Section 2: Pellet Size Selection Chart for African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)

Feeding pellets that are too large causes choking, regurgitation, and gut impaction; feeding pellets that are too small causes energy wastage and water fouling.

| Catfish Average Body Weight (g) | Life Stage | Recommended Pellet Size (mm) | Feed Type | Crude Protein % | Daily Feeding Rate (% Biomass) | Daily Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.1g – 1.0g** | Fry | 0.2 – 0.5 mm | Micro-crumble / Artemia | 50% – 56% | 15% – 20% | 4 – 6 times / day |
| **1.0g – 5.0g** | Fingerling | 0.8 – 1.2 mm | Floating mini-pellet | 48% – 52% | 8% – 10% | 3 – 4 times / day |
| **5.0g – 20g** | Post-Fingerling | 1.5 – 2.0 mm | Floating pellet | 45% – 48% | 5.0% – 7.0% | 3 times / day |
| **20g – 100g** | Juvenile / Shooter | 2.5 – 3.0 mm | Floating pellet | 42% – 45% | 3.5% – 5.0% | 2 – 3 times / day |
| **100g – 300g** | Grower | 4.0 – 4.5 mm | Floating or Extruded | 40% – 42% | 2.5% – 3.5% | 2 times / day |
| **300g – 600g** | Sub-Adult (Melange) | 6.0 mm | Floating / Sinking | 38% – 40% | 2.0% – 2.5% | 2 times / day (morning/evening) |
| **600g – 1.2kg+** | Table Size (Market) | 8.0 – 9.0 mm | Floating / Sinking | 35% – 38% | 1.2% – 1.8% | 1 – 2 times / day |
| **1.5kg – 4.0kg+** | Broodstock | 9.0 – 12.0 mm | Floating | 40% (high Vit E & C) | 1.0% | 1 time / day |

## Section 3: Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) Calculation & Diagnostic Troubleshooting

### Standard FCR Mathematical Formula:
$$\text{FCR} = \frac{\text{Total Mass of Dry Feed Supplied (kg)}}{\text{Total Net Fish Weight Gain (kg)}}$$

$$\text{Total Net Fish Weight Gain} = \text{Harvest Biomass (kg)} + \text{Mortality Biomass (kg)} - \text{Initial Stocking Biomass (kg)}$$

### Nigerian FCR Benchmarks:
- **Excellent FCR (Intensive Flow-Through / RAS):** $1.00 - 1.15$ (1.0 kg feed produces 1.0 kg fish).
- **Good / Standard FCR (Concrete Tanks & High-Density Tarpaulins):** $1.15 - 1.30$.
- **Acceptable Earthen Pond FCR:** $1.35 - 1.55$.
- **Sub-Optimal / Problematic FCR:** $> 1.65$ (Requires immediate clinical and operational audit).

### Top 5 Reasons for High FCR in Nigerian Farms:
1. **Low Dissolved Oxygen (< 4.0 mg/L):** Fish cannot efficiently synthesize protein from feed in hypoxic water.
2. **Cold Water Temperatures during Harmattan (< 23°C):** Uneaten feed rots and is wasted.
3. **Uneven Size Distribution & Cannibalism:** Larger "shooters" consume excessive feed while small fish starve.
4. **Poor Quality / Sinking Feed Disintegration:** Poorly bound on-farm sinking feed dissolves into mud within 3 minutes before fish can consume it.
5. **Parasitic & Bacterial Sub-Clinical Infection:** Chronic *Trichodina* or internal enteritis causes malabsorption in the intestinal tract.
