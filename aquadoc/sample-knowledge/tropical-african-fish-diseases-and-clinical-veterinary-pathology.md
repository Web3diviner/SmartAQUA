## Section 1: Endemic Pathologies in West and Central African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)

### 1. Cracked Head Disease / Broken Head Syndrome
- **Etiology:** A multifactorial chronic syndrome prevalent in intensive concrete and tarpaulin systems in Nigeria. Primary drivers include severe **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) deficiency**, high unionized ammonia ($NH_3$), and secondary bacterial invasion by *Edwardsiella* and *Aeromonas* species.
- **Clinical Signs:**
  1. Lateral skull bone suture inflammation followed by a visible longitudinal crack along the skull plate between the eyes and dorsal fin.
  2. Granulomatous swelling, skull hemorrhages, lethargy, and deformation of the head structure.
  3. Reduced feeding and chronic, gradual daily mortality (1–3% daily).
- **Clinical Treatment & Triage:**
  1. **Nutritional Therapy:** Supplement feed immediately with coated **Vitamin C (L-Ascorbyl-2-Polyphosphate)** at 500 – 1,000 mg per kg of feed, bound with egg white or palm oil binder for 14 consecutive days.
  2. **Water Quality Correction:** Increase daily water exchange to 40–50% to eliminate dissolved organic metabolites and keep TAN < 0.5 mg/L.
  3. **Antiseptic Salt Bath:** Administer non-iodized salt (NaCl) at **3 ppt (3 kg / 1,000 L)** in culture tanks to accelerate wound healing and prevent secondary fungal colonization.

### 2. Motile Aeromonas Septicemia (MAS) / "Red Pest" (*Aeromonas hydrophila*)
- **Etiology:** Ubiquitous Gram-negative opportunistic bacterium that becomes virulent under overcrowding, organic pollution, or rough netting handling.
- **Clinical Signs:**
  - Deep hemorrhagic skin ulcers, fin rot, inflamed vent/anus, abdominal dropsy (ascites fluid accumulation), and bilateral exophthalmia (popeye).
  - Rapid acute mortality reaching 10–20% of stock within 48–72 hours if unmanaged.
- **Clinical Action Plan:**
  1. **Immediate Feed Suspension:** Cease all feeding for 24–48 hours to minimize metabolic waste and ammonia output.
  2. **Sanitation & Flush:** Perform a 50% fresh water flush with aerated borehole water.
  3. **Veterinary Antibiotic Intervention:** Under licensed aquatic veterinary supervision, administer **Oxytetracycline** (50–75 mg/kg fish biomass/day) or **Florfenicol** (10 mg/kg biomass/day) in medicated feed for 7–10 days. Observe mandatory 21-day withdrawal period before fish harvest.

---

### 3. Columnaris Disease / "Saddleback" (*Flavobacterium columnare*)
- **Clinical Signs:** Yellowish/whitish necrotic lesions surrounding the dorsal fin ("saddleback" pattern), fraying of caudal fins, pale necrotic gills, and lethargy.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** High water temperatures (> 28°C) combined with rough grading abrasions and elevated organic load.
- **Treatment:** Add crude agricultural salt at **3 to 5 ppt** continuously in concrete tanks. For severe cases, short immersion bath in **Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$) at 2 to 4 mg/L** for 30 minutes with intensive aeration.

---

### 4. *Trichodina* & Ectoparasitic Gill Flukes (*Dactylogyrus*)
- **Clinical Signs:** Fish flash or scrape bodies against tank walls, exhibit erratic piping at surface, swollen pale gills covered in excessive mucus.
- **Microscopic Confirmation:** Wet mount skin scrape reveals circular saucer-shaped ciliates with denticular rings rotating rapidly (*Trichodina*).
- **Clinical Treatment:**
  - **Salt Immersion Bath:** Non-iodized crude salt (NaCl) at **5 ppt for 24–48 hours**, or rapid dip at **15 ppt for 5–10 minutes**.
  - **Formalin Bath (Quarantine/Broodstock):** 25–50 mg/L (ppm) formalin static bath for 60 minutes under high aeration.

## Section 2: Safe Veterinary Treatment & Dosage Reference in Nigeria

| Therapeutic Agent | Indication | Recommended Dosage | Route & Duration | Safety & Regulatory Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-Iodized Salt (NaCl)** | Osmoregulatory support, post-grading stress, *Trichodina*, *Saprolegnia*, Nitrite poisoning | **3 – 5 ppt** (3–5 kg / $m^3$) continuous bath; **10 – 15 ppt** for 5–10 min dip | Immersion bath in tanks or sorting bins | Completely safe, zero withdrawal time. Gold-standard first aid in Nigeria. |
| **Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$)** | External bacterial ulcers, Columnaris, fungal lesions | **2 – 4 mg/L (ppm)** | Static bath for 30–45 mins under continuous aeration | Water turns wine-purple, then brown. Toxic in water with low organic matter. |
| **Oxytetracycline HCl** | Systemic bacterial septicemia, severe MAS, fin rot | **50 – 75 mg / kg fish biomass / day** | Medicated feed for 7 – 10 days | 21-day withdrawal period before human consumption. Requires veterinary prescription. |
| **Florfenicol** | Acute bacterial septicemia (*Edwardsiella*, *Aeromonas*) | **10 mg / kg fish biomass / day** | Medicated feed for 10 days | Fast clearance; 14–21 day withdrawal period. |
| **Coated Vitamin C (L-Ascorbyl-2-Polyphosphate)** | Broken Head Syndrome, stress recovery, immune boosting | **500 – 1,000 mg / kg feed** | Feed top-dressed with binder for 14 – 21 days | Safe, non-toxic, highly recommended during Harmattan and post-grading. |
