## Section 1: Clinical Symptom Triage & Rapid Differential Diagnosis

When farmed fish show abnormal behavior or physical signs, immediate clinical triage is vital. The table below outlines primary clinical symptoms and their associated differential causes:

| Observed Clinical Symptom | Primary Differential Diagnosis | Secondary / Environmental Contributing Factor | Immediate Clinical First-Aid Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface piping, gasping at air-water interface | Acute Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Depletion | High Ammonia (NH3), Gill Parasites (Dactylogyrus) | Turn on aerators / paddlewheels immediately; stop feeding. |
| Complete refusal to feed (Anorexia) | Temperature drop (< 22°C), Low DO, Acute Stress | High Nitrite (NO2), Early onset of bacterial infection | Check temperature and DO; withhold feed until conditions stabilize. |
| Skin ulcers, bloody lesions, frayed fins | Motile Aeromonas Septicemia (MAS) / Columnaris | High organic load, mechanical abrasion from grading | Isolate affected pond; improve water exchange; 3–5 ppt salt dip. |
| White cotton-like patches on skin or gills | *Saprolegnia* (Fungal Infection) | Post-handling physical injury, cold water stress | Salt treatment (3–5 ppt); gently remove moribund fish. |
| Abdominal swelling (Dropsy / Ascites), protruding eyes | Bacterial Enteritis / Internal Systemic Infection | Poor feed quality, spoiled pellets, rancid fats | Suspend feeding; improve sanitation; consult aquatic veterinarian. |
| Erratic whirling, flashing against pond bottom | External Ectoparasites (*Trichodina*, *Ichthyophthirius*) | Overcrowding, high turbidity, poor water flow | Measure salinity; perform static salt bath (5 ppt for 30–60 mins). |

## Section 2: Major Bacterial Pathologies in African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)

### 1. Columnaris Disease (*Flavobacterium columnare*)
- **Clinical Signs:** "Saddleback" lesions around dorsal fin, yellowish/white necrotic patches on skin and gills, rapid fin erosion, and lethargy.
- **Predisposing Conditions:** Water temperatures above 28°C combined with high organic matter or mechanical injury from handling/grading.
- **Clinical Action:** Reduce stocking density stress. Administer prolonged salt bath (3 ppt in ponds) and maintain dissolved oxygen > 5 mg/L. Veterinary antibiotic therapy should be strictly supervised by a licensed professional.

### 2. Motile Aeromonas Septicemia (MAS) (*Aeromonas hydrophila*)
- **Clinical Signs:** Hemorrhagic septicemia, open red skin ulcers, exophthalmia (popeye), fluid accumulation in the peritoneum (dropsy), and pale liver upon necropsy.
- **Mortality Pattern:** Can cause acute mortality of 5–15% daily if left unmanaged.
- **Clinical Action:** Immediately cease feeding. Increase water exchange by 30% with fresh, oxygenated water. Disinfect all sampling nets in 200 ppm iodophor or chlorine solution.

## Section 3: Parasitic and Fungal Conditions

### 1. White Spot Disease (*Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* / "Ich")
- **Clinical Signs:** Tiny white cyst-like granules (0.5–1.0 mm) covering skin, fins, and gills. Fish rub or "flash" against pond bottoms and exhibit respiratory distress.
- **Clinical Treatment:** Raise water temperature slightly if possible (above 30°C speeds up parasite life cycle); apply sodium chloride (salt) at 3–5 ppt for 5–7 days.

### 2. *Saprolegnia* Fungal Infection
- **Clinical Signs:** Cotton-wool growths on dorsal surfaces and head. Almost always secondary to skin wounds, net abrasions, or extreme cold shock.
- **Clinical Treatment:** Sodium chloride dip (5–10 ppt for 10 minutes) or continuous bath (3 ppt). Prevent netting damage during sorting.

## Section 4: Biosecurity, Quarantine & Emergency Veterinary Protocols

1. **Isolation & Quarantine:** Any pond exhibiting unexplained mortality > 2% per 24 hours must be quarantined. No equipment (nets, buckets, graders) should be shared with healthy ponds.
2. **Mortality Disposal:** Dead fish must be removed twice daily and buried in lime pits away from water sources. Never feed mortalities to other livestock.
3. **Safe Salt Bath Protocol (Sodium Chloride):**
   - **Dip Treatment:** 10–15 ppt (10–15 kg salt per 1000 L water) for 5–10 minutes under continuous visual observation.
   - **Prolonged Static Bath:** 3–5 ppt (3–5 kg salt per 1000 L water) in concrete holding tanks or nursery ponds for 24–72 hours.
4. **Expert Escalation Triggers:**
   - Sudden mortality exceeding 5% in 24 hours.
   - Extensive deep ulcers across > 20% of sampled stock.
   - Failure of symptoms to improve within 48 hours of correcting water quality.
