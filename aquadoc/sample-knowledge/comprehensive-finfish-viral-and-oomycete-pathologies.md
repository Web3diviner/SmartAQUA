## 1. Tilapia Lake Virus (TiLV / *Tilapinevirus*) — NOTIFIABLE DISEASE
- **Pathogen:** Single-stranded, negative-sense RNA virus (Family *Amnoonviridae*).
- **Target Species:** Wild and farmed Tilapia species (*Oreochromis niloticus*, *O. mossambicus*, Red Tilapia hybrids).
- **Global & Regional Distribution:** Africa (Lake Victoria, Ghana, Egypt, Nigeria), Asia, South America.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Water temperature swings (25°C–32°C), high stocking density in cages, transport stress.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Ocular lesions: Severe corneal opacity ("cloudy eye"), endophthalmitis, eye shrinkage, or sunken eyes.
  - Skin: Extensive skin erosion, loss of scales, dark black body discoloration (melanosis), open dermal ulcers.
  - Abdomen: Moderate abdominal dropsy (ascites), lethargy, schooling near the water surface, stop feeding.
  - Internal Pathology: Syncytial giant cell hepatitis (multinucleated syncytia in liver parenchymal cells), encephalitis with perivascular cuffing in brain.
- **Mortality Profile:** Sudden catastrophic mass mortality of 20% to 90% of stock within 7–14 days. Fingerlings and juveniles are exceptionally vulnerable.
- **Biosecurity, Management & Control:**
  - **No Antibiotic Efficacy:** Antibiotics have 0% activity against viral TiLV. Administering antibiotics is useless and wasteful.
  - **Quarantine & Containment:** Immediate quarantine of the farm. Do not move live fingerlings, water, or equipment to uninfected ponds.
  - **Supportive First Aid:** Stop feeding; lower stocking density; add crude non-iodized salt (NaCl) at 3–5 ppt in tanks to assist osmoregulation.
  - **Disinfection Protocol:** Disinfect all tanks, hapas, and nets with 200–500 ppm chlorine or 2.5% Virkon Aquatic. Fallow infected ponds for at least 30 days under direct sunlight drying and quicklime ($CaO$) application at 1,000 kg/ha.

## 2. Viral Nervous Necrosis (VNN) / Viral Encephalopathy & Retinopathy (VER)
- **Pathogen:** *Betanodavirus* (Non-enveloped, positive-sense ssRNA virus).
- **Target Species:** Marine and freshwater finfish: Sea Bass, Grouper, Tilapia fry, Catfish larvae, Flounder, Turbot.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Severe neurological signs: Erratic whirling, uncontrolled corkscrew swimming, resting belly-up near the surface.
  - Swim bladder hyper-inflation (fish unable to dive, floating helplessly).
  - Microscopic Pathology: Severe vacuolation (spongiform degeneration) and necrosis in brain, spinal cord, and retina.
- **Mortality:** 80%–100% mortality in hatchery fry and early nursery fingerlings (< 30 days old).
- **Control & Therapeutics:** Strict ozone disinfection of hatchery intake water; UV sterilization of live food (rotifers/Artemia); PCR screening of broodstock; destruction of infected larval batches followed by 100 ppm iodophor disinfection.

## 3. Infectious Spleen & Kidney Necrosis Virus (ISKNV / Megalocytivirus)
- **Pathogen:** *Megalocytivirus* (Large double-stranded DNA virus, Family *Iridoviridae*).
- **Target Species:** Tilapia, Murray Cod, Sea Bass, Gourami, African Finfish.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Lethargy, pale gills (severe anemia), rapid opercular respiration, darkening of body pigmentation.
  - Internal: Massively enlarged dark-red spleen (splenomegaly) and swollen posterior kidney (renomegaly); petechial hemorrhages in visceral fat.
  - Histopathology: Characteristic hypertrophied, deeply basophilic inclusion-bearing cells throughout spleen and kidney.
- **Mortality & Control:** 30%–60% mortality. Reduce water temperature; increase dissolved oxygen; strict biosecurity quarantine.

## 4. Spring Viremia of Carp (SVC) — NOTIFIABLE DISEASE
- **Pathogen:** *Sprivivirus cyprinus* (Rhabdoviridae, ssRNA virus).
- **Target Species:** Common Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), Koi, Silver Carp, Grass Carp, Goldfish.
- **Trigger Conditions:** Water temperature between 10°C and 17°C during springtime warming or Harmattan cold transitions. Mortalities cease when water warms above 22°C.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Darkened skin, exophthalmia, pale gills with petechial bleeding, distended abdomen with yellowish mucus trailing from inflamed vent.
  - Internal: Extensive pinpoint hemorrhages on swim bladder wall, internal visceral fat, and skeletal muscle.
- **Control:** Raise water temperature above 20°C if in controlled indoor tanks; disinfect pond bottoms with quicklime ($CaO$).

## 5. Koi Herpesvirus (KHV / *Cyprinid herpesvirus 3*)
- **Pathogen:** Double-stranded DNA virus (Family *Alloherpesviridae*).
- **Target Species:** *Cyprinus carpio* (Common carp, Koi carp).
- **Temperature Window:** Active strictly between 18°C and 28°C.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Severe gill necrosis: Gills exhibit a mottled "piebald" pattern with patches of red, white, and brown rotting filaments.
  - Sunken eyes (enophthalmia), notched nose, rough sandpaper-like skin with excess mucus shedding.
  - Extremely rapid mortality: 70%–100% of stock within 48–96 hours.
- **Control:** Temperature manipulation (raising water > 30°C arrests replication); PCR quarantine certification of broodstock.

## 6. Channel Catfish Virus Disease (CCVD / *Ictalurid herpesvirus 1*)
- **Pathogen:** *Ictalurid herpesvirus 1* (Herpesviridae).
- **Target Species:** Ictalurid catfish fry and fingerlings (< 10 cm in length), occasionally African catfish hybrids under severe cold stress.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Erratic spiraling swimming, head-up hanging at water surface.
  - Distended fluid-filled belly, bulging eyes, pale gills, hemorrhages at the base of fins and barbels.
- **Control:** Reduce stocking density; maintain water temperature at optimal range; eliminate handling stress; broodstock culling.

## 7. Epizootic Ulcerative Syndrome (EUS) / Red Spot Disease — NOTIFIABLE OOMYCETE
- **Pathogen:** *Aphanomyces invadans* (Invasive pathogenic aquatic oomycete fungus).
- **Target Species:** Over 100 finfish species including African Catfish, Snakehead, Tilapia, Carp, Gourami, Mullet.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Low water temperature (18°C–25°C), low pH (< 6.2), heavy post-rain runoff, sudden drop in alkalinity.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Early stage: Pinhead red spots or raised red papules on skin.
  - Advanced stage: Massive, deep necrotizing crater-like ulcers that eat through skin, exposing white underlying muscle and skeletal bones.
  - Histopathology: Characteristic branching, non-septate fungal hyphae surrounded by extensive chronic granulomatous inflammation (granulomas).
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Immediate Alkalinity & pH Correction:** Apply Agricultural Lime ($CaCO_3$) or Hydrated Lime ($Ca(OH)_2$) to raise water pH above 7.2 and total alkalinity > 50 mg/L.
  - **Salt Immersion Bath:** Non-iodized crude salt (NaCl) at 5 ppt continuous bath in tanks for 7–10 days halts fungal sporulation.
  - **Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$):** 2–4 mg/L static bath for 60 minutes.
  - **Indigenous Bitter Leaf Extract:** Fresh aqueous Bitter Leaf extract (100 mL / 100 L water) possesses active sesquiterpene lactones that inhibit *Aphanomyces* hyphal penetration.

## 8. Saprolegniasis (Cotton Wool Disease / Water Mold)
- **Pathogen:** *Saprolegnia parasitica*, *Saprolegnia diclina* (Saprophytic oomycetes).
- **Target Species:** All finfish eggs, fry, juveniles, and adult finfish.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** Secondary infection following mechanical net abrasions, handling wounds, cold shock (< 20°C), or unfertilized dead eggs in hatcheries.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - White, gray, or brownish cotton-wool-like fluffy tufts growing on head, back, fins, or dead hatchery eggs.
  - Hyphae trap silt, turning greenish-brown; underlying skin suffers necrosis and osmoregulatory failure.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Egg Disinfection in Hatcheries:**
    * *Formalin:* 1,000–2,000 ppm dip for 15 minutes, or 250 ppm static flush for 15 minutes with aeration.
    * *Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$):* 250–500 mg/L bath for 15 minutes for fish eggs.
  - **Fish Treatment in Ponds / Holding Tanks:**
    * *Non-Iodized Salt (NaCl):* 5 to 10 ppt immersion bath for 1 hour, or 3 ppt continuous bath in holding tanks.
    * *Potassium Permanganate:* 2 mg/L static bath for 60 minutes.

## 9. Branchiomycosis (Gill Rot Disease)
- **Pathogen:** *Branchiomyces sanguinis*, *Branchiomyces demigrans* (Fungal vascular parasites).
- **Target Species:** Common Carp, Catfish, Tilapia, Pike.
- **Predisposing Triggers:** High water temperature (> 25°C–30°C), severe organic pollution, heavy unionized ammonia, high algal bloom die-offs.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Fungal hyphae grow inside the blood vessels of gill filaments, causing thrombosis, blood flow obstruction, and localized infarction (ischemic tissue death).
  - Gills appear mottled, marbled, with alternating streaks of red (hyperemic), white (ischemic), and gray-brown (necrotic sloughing).
  - Fish display extreme respiratory piping at the surface and suffocate rapidly.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Urgent Sanitation:** Immediately stop feeding; pump out bottom sediment sludge; perform 50% fresh water flush.
  - Apply Quicklime ($CaO$) at 150–200 kg/ha to pond water to disinfect organic spore reservoirs.
  - Copper sulfate ($CuSO_4$) at 0.5–1.0 mg/L (strictly adjusted for water hardness) or salt at 3–5 ppt.
