## Section 1: Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Dynamics & Emergency Management

### Critical DO Ranges for African Catfish & Nile Tilapia
- **Optimal Range:** 5.0 – 8.0 mg/L (Promotes maximum feed conversion and immune competence).
- **Stress Zone:** 3.0 – 4.9 mg/L (Feed intake drops significantly, FCR worsens).
- **Critical Hypoxia:** < 3.0 mg/L (Fish crowd near water surface, pipe for air, cessation of feeding).
- **Lethal Threshold:** < 1.0 mg/L for prolonged periods (Mortality occurs, especially in early morning before dawn).

### Emergency Protocol for Oxygen Depletion:
1. **Activate Emergency Aeration:** Turn on paddlewheels, aspirators, or diffused air stones immediately.
2. **Immediate Feed Suspension:** Cease all feeding. Digestion consumes substantial oxygen; feeding hypoxic fish causes rapid mortality.
3. **Fresh Water Exchange:** Pump in fresh, oxygen-rich water from source boreholes or reservoirs at the surface level.

## Section 2: Ammonia (TAN) & Nitrite (NO2) Toxicity

### Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN) vs. Toxic Free Ammonia (NH3)
- **Mechanism:** Ammonia exists in two forms in equilibrium: non-toxic ammonium ion ($NH_4^+$) and toxic unionized ammonia ($NH_3$).
- **pH and Temperature Effect:** As water pH and temperature rise, the proportion of toxic $NH_3$ increases dramatically:
  - At pH 7.0 and 28°C: ~0.7% of TAN is toxic $NH_3$.
  - At pH 8.5 and 28°C: ~15% of TAN is toxic $NH_3$ (over 20 times more toxic!).
- **Safe Limit:** Toxic $NH_3$ must remain below **0.05 mg/L**; levels above **0.2 mg/L** cause severe gill burning and death.

### Nitrite ($NO_2^-$) and "Brown Blood Disease"
- Nitrite oxidizes hemoglobin into methemoglobin, which cannot transport oxygen, turning the fish's blood chocolate brown.
- **Treatment:** Add non-iodized salt (NaCl) at 1–2 kg per 1000 liters (1–2 ppt). Chloride ions ($Cl^-$) competitively block nitrite uptake across fish gills.

## Section 3: Temperature Dynamics & Metabolic Q10 Scaling

- African catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*) have a biological temperature comfort zone between **26°C and 30°C**.
- **Temperature Drop (< 22°C):** Metabolic rate and digestive enzymatic activity drop by ~50% per the $Q_{10}=2.0$ metabolic rule. Fish refuse to consume feed. Supplying unconsumed pellets leads to water fouling and ammonia spikes.
- **High Temperature (> 32°C):** Oxygen solubility in water decreases while fish oxygen demand peaks, creating sudden risk of night-time suffocation.
