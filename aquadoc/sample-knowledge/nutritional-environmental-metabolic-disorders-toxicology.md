## 1. Nutritional & Metabolic Deficiency Syndromes

### 1.1 Broken Head Syndrome / Cracked Skull Disease (Severe Ascorbic Acid / Vitamin C Deficiency)
- **Etiology:** Nutritional failure where feed lacks adequate stable, coated **L-Ascorbic Acid** (< 100 mg/kg feed), aggravated by warm water oxidation and high dissolved ammonia. Vitamin C is a mandatory cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase in collagen and cartilage synthesis.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Progressive inflammation across the junction of cranial skull bones.
  - A prominent vertical or transverse crack ruptures the skull plate between the eyes and dorsal crest.
  - Granulomatous, spongy hemorrhagic tissue extrudes through the open skull fissure.
  - Opercular gill covers curl backward (operculum short cover deformity), exposing red gill filaments.
  - Spinal deformities: Lordosis (swayback) and scoliosis (lateral spinal curvature).
  - Reduced feeding, emaciation, and gradual daily mortality of 1%–3%.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Immediate In-Feed Dietary Therapy:** Top-dress feed with **Coated Vitamin C (L-Ascorbyl-2-Polyphosphate / Stay-C)** at **500 to 1,000 mg per 1 kg feed (0.5–1.0 g/kg)**. Use a lipid binder (crude palm oil, fish oil, or raw egg white at 10–20 mL/kg) to ensure the supplement adheres to floating pellets.
  - **Treatment Duration:** Feed daily for **14 to 21 consecutive days**. Skull lesions begin re-calcifying and forming scar tissue within 7–10 days.
  - **Water Quality Remediation:** Perform 40%–50% water exchange to eliminate toxic unionized ammonia ($NH_3 < 0.05\text{ mg/L}$).
  - **Antiseptic Tank Bath:** Apply non-iodized salt (NaCl) at **3 to 5 ppt (3–5 kg per 1,000 L)** to prevent secondary *Saprolegnia* fungal and *Aeromonas* bacterial infection of the open skull wound.

### 1.2 Fatty Liver Disease / Hepatic Lipidosis & Steatosis
- **Etiology:** Feeding rancid, oxidized, or stale feeds; improper storage exposed to tropical heat/sunlight; excessive dietary carbohydrate or digestible energy-to-protein ratio ($E:P$ imbalance); choline and methionine deficiency.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Sluggish, bloated fish; swollen yellowish, pale, greasy liver upon necropsy with rounded margins; liver tissue floats when placed in water (due to high fat content).
  - Chronic low-grade mortalities, sudden death during net handling or routine transport.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Dietary Purge:** Immediately discard spoiled/oxidized feed bags. Withhold feed for 48–72 hours.
  - **Dietary Reformulation:** Switch to fresh, high-protein feed (> 42% CP for catfish) with fresh lipid sources.
  - **Antioxidant Supplementation:** Supplement feed with **Vitamin E (Alpha-Tocopherol)** at **150–200 mg/kg feed**, **Vitamin C** at **500 mg/kg**, and **Choline Chloride** at **1,000 mg/kg** for 14 days to metabolize hepatic triglycerides.

### 1.3 Aflatoxicosis (Mycotoxin Poisoning)
- **Etiology:** Ingestion of feed contaminated with **Aflatoxins ($B_1, B_2, G_1, G_2$)** produced by the storage molds *Aspergillus flavus* and *Aspergillus parasiticus* in humid tropical climates.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Severe anorexia (refusal to feed), pale yellow-brown liver with multiple nodular hepatomas (liver tumors), severe anemia, ascites, and impaired blood clotting.
  - Suppressed immune response leading to widespread secondary bacterial ulcers (*MAS*).
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Destroy Feed:** Immediately remove and destroy mouldy feed batches. Aflatoxins are heat-stable and cannot be cooked away.
  - **Mycotoxin Binders:** Include aluminosilicate clay / bentonite or yeast cell wall (Mannan-oligosaccharides / MOS) in feed at **2–5 g/kg feed** to bind ingested toxins.
  - Feed fresh feed supplemented with Vitamin C (500 mg/kg) and herbal hepatoprotectants (Milk thistle extract / Silymarin at 100 mg/kg).

---

### 1.4 Nutritional Myopathy / "Broken Back Syndrome" (Vitamin E & Selenium Deficiency)
- **Clinical Symptoms:** Necrosis of lateral skeletal trunk muscle, rigid spinal column, inability to flex body for swimming, lateral spinal fractures ("broken back").
- **Therapeutics:** Supplement feed with Vitamin E (200 mg/kg) and organic selenium (0.3 mg/kg) for 14 days.

## 2. Environmental & Water Quality Intoxications

### 2.1 Brown Blood Disease / Nitrite Poisoning (Methemoglobinemia)
- **Etiology:** High nitrite concentration ($NO_2^- > 0.5\text{ mg/L}$) resulting from biofilter failure, incomplete nitrification, or low dissolved oxygen in intensive tanks. Nitrite oxidizes hemoglobin's ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$) into ferric iron ($Fe^{3+}$), forming **methemoglobin**, which cannot bind or transport oxygen.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Gills appear dark chocolate-brown or muddy brown rather than bright healthy red.
  - Blood collected from the caudal vessel is dark chocolate-brown and does not turn red upon exposure to air.
  - Fish show extreme respiratory distress (piping, gasping at surface, gathering at aerator inflows) even when dissolved oxygen levels in the water are adequate.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Chloride Ion Therapy (The Salt Antidote):**
    * Add non-iodized sodium chloride (NaCl) or calcium chloride ($CaCl_2$) immediately.
    * **Target Chloride Ratio:** Maintain a **Chloride to Nitrite ratio ($Cl^- : NO_2^-$) of at least 6:1 (preferably 10:1)**.
    * **Field Rule of Thumb:** Apply **3 to 5 ppt crude salt (3–5 kg per 1,000 L / $m^3$ water)**. The chloride ($Cl^-$) ions compete with nitrite ($NO_2^-$) at the gill gill-lamellae transport pumps, blocking nitrite uptake completely.
  - **Water Flush & Aeration:** Perform a 50% fresh water flush; maximize aeration.

---

### 2.2 Ammonia Poisoning / Unionized Ammonia ($NH_3$) Toxicity
- **Etiology:** Unionized free ammonia ($NH_3 > 0.05\text{ mg/L}$), exponentially exacerbated by high pH (> 8.2) and warm water temperatures (> 28°C).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Hyperexcitability, erratic darting, crashing into tank walls, tremors, and terminal tetanic muscle spasms.
  - Severe gill hyperplasia: Gills appear swollen, congested, with fused filaments and excess thick mucus.
  - Subcutaneous hemorrhages around eyes, ventral abdomen, and fin bases.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - **Immediate Feed Cut:** Stop all feeding immediately (feed digestion generates > 80% of total ammonia).
  - **Immediate Water Exchange:** Drain 50%–70% of bottom water (where ammonia accumulates) and replenish with fresh borehole water.
  - **pH Lowering:** If pH > 8.5, safely lower water pH to 7.0–7.4 using organic humic acids, unrefined agricultural molasses (carbon source to stimulate heterotrophic bacterial uptake), or small amounts of dissolved alum ($Al_2(SO_4)_3$).
  - **Zeolite / Biofloc Remediation:** In RAS, add clinoptilolite zeolite (50–100 g/$m^3$) or add molasses at a 15:1 Carbon-to-Nitrogen ($C:N$) ratio.

---

### 2.3 Gas Bubble Disease (Gas Supersaturation)
- **Etiology:** Total dissolved gas pressure (TGP) exceeding 110%–115%, caused by faulty pump plumbing (air leaks on suction side of water pump), rapid heating of cold deep borehole water, or intense microalgae oxygen supersaturation on sunny afternoons ($DO > 200\%$).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Tiny gas bubbles (emboli) clearly visible in the eye orbit (exophthalmia), inside fin membranes, along lateral line, and blocking gill capillaries.
  - Fish float uncontrollably at the surface or swim with tail pointing upward.
- **Clinical Therapeutics & Curative Protocols:**
  - Vigorous degassing: Cascade incoming water over splash boards, cascade towers, or venturi degassers before entering tanks.
  - Inspect pump intake pipes for cracked seals or suction air leaks.

---

### 2.4 Acute Hypoxia / Dissolved Oxygen Depletion ($DO < 2.0\text{ mg/L}$)
- **Clinical Symptoms:** Entire fish population piping at the water-air interface at dawn, mouth gasping, wide opercular flaring, sudden death with mouths agape and gills pale.
- **Curative Protocol:** Emergency paddlewheel activation; turn on backup aerators; pump fresh oxygenated water; immediately stop feeding; apply liquid oxygen or pure oxygen injection if equipped.

---

### 2.5 Acid Shock & Alkalosis (Extreme pH Disorders)
- **Acid Shock ($pH < 5.5$):**
  * *Signs:* Gills turn dark brown, excess coagulated mucus, skin corrosion, lethargy.
  * *Cure:* Broadcast Agricultural Limestone ($CaCO_3$) or Hydrated Lime at **20–50 g/$m^3$** slowly until pH stabilizes at 7.0–7.5.
- **Alkali Burn ($pH > 9.0$):**
  * *Signs:* Frayed fin margins, lens cataract cloudiness, eye corrosion.
  * *Cure:* Stop liming; partial water exchange; add molasses or agricultural gypsum ($CaSO_4$).

---

### 2.6 Harmattan & Cold Temperature Shock (< 20°C in Tropical Finfish)
- **Clinical Signs:** Complete anorexia, sluggish bottom-huddling, loss of equilibrium, high vulnerability to *Saprolegnia* water mold.
- **Curative Protocol:** Reduce daily feeding ration by 50%–75% (metabolism follows deterministic Q10 drop); feed warm-temperature digestible floating pellets; add crude salt at 3 ppt; use poly-tunnels or greenhouse tarpaulins over outdoor tanks.
