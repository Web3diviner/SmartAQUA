## 1. Protozoan Ciliates & Flagellates

### 1.1 White Spot Disease / "Ich" (*Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* in Freshwater; *Cryptocaryon irritans* in Marine)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Obligate ciliated protozoan with a complex 3-stage life cycle: feeding stage under skin (*Trophont*), encysted dividing stage on substrate (*Tomont*), and free-swimming infectious theronts (*Theronts*).
- **Target Species:** All freshwater finfish (Catfish, Tilapia, Carp, Ornamental finfish).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Distinct white pinpoint cysts (0.5–1.0 mm) like salt grains scattered over skin, fins, cornea, and gills.
  - "Flashing" behavior: Fish rapidly scratch and rub sides against pond bottoms and tank walls.
  - Severe respiratory distress, clamped fins, and lethargy.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Temperature Acceleration:* Raising water temperature to 30°C–32°C accelerates the parasite life cycle and kills heat-sensitive theronts.
  - *Salt Treatment (NaCl):* Non-iodized crude salt at **3 to 5 ppt (3–5 kg per 1,000 L)** continuous immersion in concrete tanks for 7 to 10 days. Osmotic pressure destroys free-swimming theronts before they re-infect.
  - *Formalin Bath:* 25 ppm (25 mL / 1,000 L) static bath for 24 hours, or 150–250 ppm for 30–45 minutes with continuous aeration.

### 1.2 *Trichodina* & Peritrichous Ciliates (*Trichodinella*, *Tripartiella*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Disc-shaped circular ciliates with a striking internal toothed ring of denticles that abrade epithelial cells.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Bluish-white mucus film over skin and head; frayed fins; excessive opercular flapping.
  - Fish show extreme irritation, piping at surface, and loss of appetite.
  - Massive mortalities in catfish nursery fry (< 5g) and fingerlings.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Salt Bath:* **5 ppt for 24–48 hours** in tanks or **15 ppt for 5–10 minutes** as a rapid dip.
  - *Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$):* 2 to 4 mg/L (ppm) bath for 45 minutes with high aeration.
  - *Botanical Bath:* Fresh aqueous Bitter Leaf extract (50–100 mL / 100 L water) destroys *Trichodina* membranes within 24 hours.

---

### 1.3 Velvet Disease / Gold Dust Disease / Rust Disease (*Piscinoodinium pillulare* / *Amyloodinium ocellatum*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Photosynthetic parasitic dinoflagellates with rhizoids that penetrate deep into host epithelial cells.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Fine velvety, yellowish-gold or rust-colored dust-like sheen visible under angled light across skin and fins.
  - Severe skin peeling, rapid gill pumping, flashing, and sudden mass mortality.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Copper Sulfate ($CuSO_4$):* 0.15–0.2 mg/L free copper ion continuous bath for 10–14 days (strictly monitor water hardness; do NOT use in soft water with total alkalinity < 50 mg/L).
  - *Salt Therapy:* 5 ppt salt bath combined with total darkness for 48 hours (inhibits photosynthetic dinospores).

---

### 1.4 *Costia* / Slime Disease (*Ichthyobodo necator*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Tiny, bean-shaped flagellates with a characteristic flicking, corkscrew motility under microscope ($400\times$).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Excessive bluish-gray slime/mucus production ("Slime Disease"); patchy grayish skin lesions.
  - Severe gill destruction and asphyxiation in fingerlings.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Formalin:* 25 ppm continuous bath or 150 ppm for 45 minutes.
  - *Salt:* 5 to 10 ppt bath for 60 minutes.

---

### 1.5 *Chilodonella* Infestation (*Chilodonella piscicola*, *Chilodonella hexasticha*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Heart-shaped or leaf-shaped ciliated protozoan that thrives in cool to temperate water (10°C–20°C).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Extensive cloudiness of skin, notched dorsal fin, severe respiratory suffocation.
  - Causes severe "winter kills" or Harmattan season losses.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - 3–5 ppt salt bath for 5 days or 2 ppm $KMnO_4$ bath.

---

### 1.6 Red Sore Disease (*Epistylis* & *Heteropolaria*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Stalked colonial peritrichous ciliates that attach to skin and eroded bone, creating a base for secondary *Aeromonas* ulcers.
- **Clinical Signs:** Fluffy, whitish fungus-like tufts on red, eroded scales and skull bones (often confused with *Saprolegnia*).
- **Therapeutics:** 3 ppt salt + 2 ppm $KMnO_4$ bath.

---

### 1.7 Hexamitiasis / "Hole-in-the-Head" (*Spironucleus vortens*, *Hexamita salmonis*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Flagellated protozoans colonizing gallbladder and intestinal lumen.
- **Clinical Signs:** White stringy feces, emaciation, deep pitting sensory pore craters on head and lateral line.
- **Therapeutics:** *Metronidazole* at 50 mg per kg fish body weight in medicated feed for 5–7 days, or 10 mg/L bath in aquarium systems.

## 2. Monogenean Trematodes (Flukes)

### 2.1 Gill Flukes (*Dactylogyrus spp.*)
- **Identification:** Monogenean trematode with 4 prominent anterior eye spots and a posterior haptor with large anchoring hooks (anchors). Oviparous (egg-laying).
- **Location:** Strictly on gill filaments.
- **Clinical Symptoms:** Gills flared, swollen, mottled, covered with mucus; rapid opercular movements; fish gasping near surface.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Praziquantel:* 2 to 5 mg/L (ppm) bath in tanks for 24 hours, or 10 mg/L for 3 hours; repeated after 5 days to kill newly hatched eggs. Or in-feed *Praziquantel* at 40 mg/kg fish biomass/day for 3 days.
  - *Formalin:* 25–30 ppm static bath.
  - *Salt (NaCl):* 10–15 ppt short dip for 10 minutes dislodges adult flukes.

### 2.2 Skin Flukes (*Gyrodactylus spp.*)
- **Identification:** No eye spots; viviparous (gives birth to live young; embryos visible inside parent fluke like Russian nesting dolls).
- **Location:** Skin, fins, and cornea.
- **Clinical Symptoms:** Clamped fins, localized skin hemorrhages, excess mucus shedding, flashing.
- **Therapeutics:** Praziquantel at 2–4 mg/L or static salt bath at 5 ppt for 48 hours (single treatment often curative since no eggs are laid).

## 3. Parasitic Crustacea (Lernaeids & Branchiurans)

### 3.1 Anchor Worm (*Lernaea cyprinacea*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Modified parasitic copepod. Adult female embeds branched anchor-like cephalic horn deep into fish muscle, leaving trailing ribbon-like egg sacs hanging externally.
- **Target Species:** Carps, Catfish, Tilapia, Cichlids.
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Visible worm-like structures (10–15 mm long) protruding from skin, fin bases, or mouth.
  - Deep focal hemorrhagic ulceration and secondary fungal/*Aeromonas* infections at the penetration site.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Diflubenzuron / Lufenuron (Chitin Synthesis Inhibitors):* 0.03–0.05 mg/L (ppm) static bath in ponds. Destroys molting larvae (copepodids) over 14 days.
  - *Manual Removal (Broodstock):* Gently pull adult females with forceps and dab wound with Povidone-Iodine solution, followed by a 3 ppt salt bath.
  - *Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$):* 4–5 mg/L bath for 30 minutes kills free-swimming larval stages.

---

### 3.2 Fish Louse (*Argulus foliaceus*, *Argulus japonicus*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Disc-shaped, flat, greenish-translucent branchiuran crustacean (5–10 mm) with two large suckers and a poisonous pre-oral stinging spine (stylet).
- **Clinical Symptoms & Pathological Signs:**
  - Visible lice crawling over fish flanks and fins.
  - Intense irritation, jumping out of water, localized red puncture ulcers, severe anemia in fry.
  - Argulus injects cytolytic venom, causing localized tissue necrosis.
- **Therapeutics & Curative Protocol:**
  - *Trichlorfon / Organophosphates:* 0.25–0.5 mg/L (ppm) pond treatment (use with extreme caution; toxic to humans and birds).
  - *Emamectin Benzoate:* 50 mcg/kg fish biomass/day in feed for 7 days.
  - *Salt Dip:* 15–20 ppt salt dip for 10 minutes causes adult lice to detach.

---

### 3.3 Gill Maggots (*Ergasilus sieboldi*)
- **Pathogen Biology:** Parasitic cyclopoid copepods where females use enlarged second antennae modified into claw-like hooks to grasp gill filaments.
- **Clinical Signs:** White maggot-like bodies clinging to gill filaments; severe gill erosion, suffocation, and anemia.
- **Therapeutics:** Diflubenzuron at 0.05 mg/L or continuous 5 ppt salt bath.

---

## 4. Intestinal Helminths & Nematodes

### 4.1 Red Worm / Camallanus Nematodes (*Camallanus cotti*)
- **Clinical Symptoms:** Thread-like bright red worms protruding from the fish's vent/anus when resting. Severe intestinal inflammation, swollen belly, or emaciation.
- **Therapeutics:**
  - *Levamisole HCl:* 2 to 4 mg/L in bath for 24 hours, followed by 50% water exchange and substrate vacuuming; or in-feed at 10 mg/kg biomass for 2 days.
  - *Fenbendazole / Flubendazole:* In-feed at 25–50 mg/kg biomass for 5 days.
  - *Pawpaw (*Carica papaya*) Seed Powder:* In-feed at 3–5 g per kg feed for 7 days (contains natural papain and carpaine anthelmintics).
