## Section 1: Broodstock Selection and Maturity Conditioning

High fry survival and fertilization rates in African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*) depend directly on broodstock health, genetic lineage, and maturity assessment:

### 1. Female Broodstock Selection (Target Weight: 1.5 – 3.0 kg)
- **Visual Indicators:** Well-rounded, soft, distended abdomen extending past the pelvic fins; reddish/vascularized, swollen genital papilla.
- **Cannulation Biopsy Test:** Insert a sterile plastic cannula catheter (1.2 mm diameter) gently into the genital pore for 2–3 cm, aspirate a small sample of oocytes. Mature eggs appear uniform in size (1.1 – 1.3 mm diameter), greenish-brown to olive in color, with non-sticky spherical margins and polarized nuclei.

### 2. Male Broodstock Selection (Target Weight: 1.5 – 2.5 kg)
- **Visual Indicators:** Elongated, pointed, rigid genital papilla with a distinctly reddish or pinkish tip.
- **Conditioning Protocol:** House males and females in separate concrete tanks at 27°C – 29°C. Feed a high-protein broodstock diet (40% – 45% crude protein, supplemented with 500 mg/kg Vitamin C and 100 mg/kg Vitamin E) at 1.5% of body weight daily for at least 4 weeks prior to induction.

## Section 2: Hormone Induced Ovulation and Latency Calculation

### 1. Synthetic Hormone (*Ovulin* / *Ovaprim* / sGnRH-a + Domperidone)
- **Dosage Female:** **0.50 mL per 1.0 kg** body weight.
- **Dosage Male:** **0.25 mL per 1.0 kg** body weight (if stripped or kept for multiple strippings).
- **Injection Technique:** Intramuscular injection at a 45° angle into the dorsal muscle midway between the dorsal fin and lateral line, pointing toward the tail. Depress plunger smoothly and massage injection site for 5 seconds to prevent hormone leakage.

### 2. Latency Period (Time from Injection to Stripping)
Latency duration is inversely proportional to water temperature:
| Water Temperature (°C) | Latency Duration (Hours) | Stripping Readiness Time |
|---|---|---|
| 25°C | 13 – 14 hours | Morning (09:00 if injected at 20:00) |
| 26°C | 11 – 12 hours | Morning (08:00 if injected at 20:00) |
| 27°C | 10 – 11 hours | Early Morning (07:00 if injected at 20:00) |
| **28°C – 29°C (Optimal)** | **9 – 10 hours** | **Early Morning (06:00 if injected at 20:00)** |
| 30°C | 7 – 8 hours | Night / Dawn (04:00 if injected at 20:00) |

> **Critical Warning:** Stripping too early yields unripe eggs with central nuclei that fail to fertilize. Stripping too late (> 1.5 hours past latency peak) causes over-ripening, egg lysis, waterlogging, and total hatch failure.

## Section 3: Egg Stripping, Milt Extraction, and Dry Fertilization

1. **Anesthesia & Drying:** Anesthetize the injected female in clove oil (40 mg/L) or MS-222. Dry the vent and abdominal area completely with a clean towel; water prematurely activates and closes the egg micropyle.
2. **Egg Stripping:** Apply gentle, rhythmic thumb pressure along the female flank from below the pectoral fins toward the genital pore into a clean, dry plastic or stainless-steel bowl.
3. **Milt Extraction:** Euthanize selected male humanely; dissect the abdominal cavity to excise the bilobed testes. Rinse testes in 0.9% physiological saline ($NaCl$); make fine cross-incisions with a sterile scalpel blade and squeeze the concentrated creamy-white sperm directly over the stripped eggs.
4. **Feather Mixing:** Mix eggs and milt gently using a dry, clean bird feather for 30–60 seconds before adding water.
5. **Water Activation:** Add clean hatchery water (equal volume to eggs) and swirl for 60 seconds. The sperm is activated for 60–90 seconds to fertilize the eggs.

## Section 4: Incubation, Hatching, and Nursery Management

### 1. Incubation Setup
- Spread fertilized eggs in a single layer over synthetic 1.0 mm mesh incubation nets (kakabans) submerged 3–5 cm below the surface in shallow flow-through troughs (water depth 15–20 cm).
- Maintain gentle continuous flow-through (0.5 – 1.0 L/min) with constant aeration. Dissolved oxygen must remain > 6.0 mg/L at 27°C – 29°C.
- Hatching completes in **22 – 26 hours**. Free-swimming hatchlings pass through the mesh to the trough bottom; immediately remove the mesh net and unhatched opaque/white dead eggs to prevent *Saprolegnia* fungal infestation.

### 2. Live Food and Larval Feeding Protocol (Days 1 to 21)
| Larval Age | Feed Type | Particle Size | Feeding Frequency | Management Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Days 1 – 3** (0–72h) | Endogenous Yolk Sac | None | None | Keep water clean; darkness reduces larval hyperactivity. |
| **Days 3 – 8** | Decapsulated *Artemia* cysts or live *Artemia nauplii* | 150 – 250 μm | Every 3 hours (6–8 times daily) | First external feeding when 80% yolk sac is absorbed. |
| **Days 9 – 14** | Micro-starter crumb (55% Crude Protein) + *Moina* / *Daphnia* | 0.2 – 0.3 mm | Every 4 hours (5–6 times daily) | Siphon tank bottom uneaten feed twice daily; 40% water exchange. |
| **Days 15 – 21** | High-protein starter crumble (50% CP) | 0.3 – 0.5 mm | 4 times daily | First size-grading on Day 15 to separate early shooters. |
| **Days 22 – 35** | 0.8 mm – 1.2 mm micro-pellets (45%–48% CP) | 0.8 – 1.2 mm | 3 times daily | Grade every 4–5 days. Stocking density: 500–800 fingerlings/$m^2$. |

### 3. Cannibalism Prevention Strategy
- African catfish are highly predatory. Unchecked "shooters" (fast-growing individuals) consume 10–25 smaller siblings per day.
- **Grading Schedule:** Mechanically grade fingerlings using plastic/aluminum bar graders every 4 to 5 days from Day 14 to Day 35. Separate into 3 distinct size categories: Small (Runts), Medium (Uniform Batch), and Large (Jumpers).
- Maintain consistent dim lighting or shade netting over nursery tanks to minimize aggressive territorial biting.
