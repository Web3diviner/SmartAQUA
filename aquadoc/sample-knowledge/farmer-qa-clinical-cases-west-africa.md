## Case 1: Catfish Gathering / Crowding Around the Water Inlet Pipe

**Farmer Question:**  
*"Why are my African catfish in Pond 2 rushing to cluster tightly around the incoming water pipe or splashing aerator at the surface?"*

**Veterinary Diagnostic Analysis:**  
When catfish crowd near the water inlet or splash zone, they are exhibiting an emergency behavioral response to **Acute Dissolved Oxygen Depletion (Hypoxia)** or **High Unionized Ammonia / Toxic Hydrogen Sulfide ($H_2S$)** in the main water body. The fresh incoming water represents the only zone with sufficient oxygen and lower toxicant concentration.

**Clinical Recommendations:**
1. **Immediate Aeration:** Turn on all available mechanical aerators, paddlewheels, or increase the freshwater inflow rate immediately.
2. **Immediate Feed Stoppage:** Withhold all feed until dissolved oxygen returns above 5.0 mg/L. Digesting protein increases fish oxygen consumption by 40% (Specific Dynamic Action).
3. **Pond Sludge Drainage:** Open bottom drain valves to flush deoxygenated organic sediment.

## Case 2: Post-Downpour Fingerling Die-Off in Concrete & Tarpaulin Tanks

**Farmer Question:**  
*"After a heavy thunderstorm last night, 200 of my 10g catfish post-fingerlings died this morning. What happened and how do I prevent this?"*

**Veterinary Diagnostic Analysis:**  
Sudden mortality following heavy tropical rainstorms is caused by two compounding factors:
1. **Thermal Shock & Cold Downpour Turnover:** Rainwater in Nigeria can be 18°C–20°C, rapidly cooling surface water and dropping tank temperature below the catfish comfort threshold (26°C–30°C).
2. **Acid Rain Runoff & pH Crash:** Rainfall in coastal and rainforest regions is naturally acidic (pH 5.0–5.5). In unbuffered tanks, this drops water pH rapidly, damaging juvenile gill lamellae and causing acute respiratory failure.

**Clinical Recommendations:**
1. **Buffer Tanks with Agricultural Lime ($CaCO_3$):** Maintain Total Alkalinity > 60 mg/L by adding agricultural limestone to buffer incoming rainwater.
2. **Install Protective Tank Covers:** Use 70% shading nets or transparent greenhouse polythene sheeting over outdoor tarpaulins and nursery tanks.
3. **Emergency Salt Bath:** Apply non-iodized crude salt (NaCl) at **3 ppt (3 kg per 1,000 liters)** to stabilize osmotic pressure and restore gill ion exchange.

## Case 3: Water Smells Like Rotten Eggs ($H_2S$ Toxicity)

**Farmer Question:**  
*"The water at the bottom of my catfish pond has turned dark blackish and smells like rotten eggs. My fish are not feeding and look sluggish."*

**Veterinary Diagnostic Analysis:**  
The rotten egg odor indicates **Hydrogen Sulfide ($H_2S$)** gas production by anaerobic sulfate-reducing bacteria in decomposing organic bottom sludge (accumulated feces and uneaten feed). Un-ionized $H_2S$ is lethal to fish at concentrations as low as **0.01 mg/L**, causing cellular asphyxiation even when dissolved oxygen appears adequate.

**Clinical Recommendations:**
1. **Bottom Sludge Siphoning / Flushing:** Drain and siphon out bottom anaerobic sludge immediately.
2. **Intense Aeration:** $H_2S$ is volatile and rapidly oxidizes into non-toxic sulfate ($SO_4^{2-}$) in the presence of vigorous aeration.
3. **Apply Agricultural Lime ($CaCO_3$) / Zeolite:** Raise pH above 7.5. At higher pH, toxic $H_2S$ dissociates into non-toxic bisulfide ($HS^-$) ions.

## Case 4: Salt Dosage and Usage in Commercial Tanks

**Farmer Question:**  
*"How much non-iodized salt should I put in my 1,000-liter concrete or tarpaulin fish tank for stress and parasite prevention?"*

**Veterinary Standard Dosage Protocol:**
- **Routine Anti-Stress & Post-Grading Bath (Continuous in tank):**  
  **3 to 5 kg of non-iodized crude salt per 1,000 Liters (3 to 5 ppt)**.  
  *Purpose:* Reduces osmoregulatory energy expenditure by 30%, repairs skin mucus, and prevents fungal (*Saprolegnia*) and bacterial colonization on grading wounds.
- **Short Therapeutic Dip (for active external parasites like *Trichodina*):**  
  **10 to 15 kg per 1,000 Liters (10 to 15 ppt) for 5 to 10 minutes** in a separate sorting bin under aeration. Return fish immediately to fresh water if they lose equilibrium.
- **Nitrite Toxicity ($NO_2^-$) Protection:**  
  **1 to 2 kg per 1,000 Liters (1 to 2 ppt)**. Chloride ions ($Cl^-$) competitively inhibit toxic nitrite uptake across gill membranes, preventing "Brown Blood Disease" (methemoglobinemia).
