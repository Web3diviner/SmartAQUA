## Section 1: Overview of Aquaculture Systems in Nigeria and West Africa

Aquaculture in Nigeria and the West/Central African sub-region (Ghana, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, Togo) is predominantly centered on the **African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)**, **Heterobranchus longifilis**, and their hardy hybrid **Heteroclarias ("Dutch" / hybrid catfish)**, followed by the **Nile Tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*)**.

### Major Culture Systems in Nigeria:
1. **Concrete Flow-Through Tanks (Intensive Urban/Semi-Urban Systems):**
   - High stocking density: 50 to 120 fish per $m^2$ (or 100 to 200 fish per $m^3$).
   - Requires daily continuous or intermittent flow-through exchange (20% to 50% daily water refresh).
   - High reliance on commercial extruded floating feeds (42% to 45% crude protein).
2. **Earthen Ponds (Extensive / Semi-Intensive Rural Systems):**
   - Common in Delta, Ogun, Benue, Kaduna, and Edo state floodplains.
   - Stocking density: 5 to 15 fish per $m^2$.
   - Primary management factors: liming acidic laterite soils, pond bottom desilting, predator netting, and natural bloom fertilization.
3. **Tarpaulin / High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) Collapsible Tanks:**
   - Popular for backyard and peri-urban smallholders in Lagos, Ibadan, Abuja, and Port Harcourt.
   - Stocking density: 40 to 80 fish per $m^3$.
   - Vulnerable to rapid daytime heating and nighttime temperature swings; shading nets (70% shade) are mandatory.

## Section 2: Water Sourcing & Pre-Treatment in West Africa

### Underground Borehole Water Dynamics:
- **Low Dissolved Oxygen & High Dissolved Gaseous CO2:** Deep borehole water across southern and northern Nigeria typically emerges with **0.0 to 1.5 mg/L Dissolved Oxygen**. Borehole water must **never be pumped directly into stocked fish tanks**.
- **Degassing & Splash Aeration Cascade:** Water must pass through a perforated spray header, cascade aerator, or open overhead holding tank to absorb atmospheric oxygen ($\ge 5.0\text{ mg/L}$) and vent volatile dissolved gases.
- **Iron Oxidation:** Many Nigerian aquifers contain soluble ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$). Aeration oxidizes iron into insoluble ferric hydroxide ($Fe(OH)_3$), which appears as rusty reddish-brown deposits. Water should settle in a reservoir before entering production tanks to prevent gill fouling.
- **Acidity & Liming:** Borehole water in coastal and rainforest belts (Lagos, Ogun, Edo, Rivers) often has a naturally low pH of **5.2 to 6.2**. Agricultural lime ($CaCO_3$) or calcium hydroxide ($Ca(OH)_2$) should be added to the header reservoir to buffer pH to the optimal range of **6.8 to 7.8**.

## Section 3: Stocking, Size Grading & Cannibalism Prevention in *Clarias*

African catfish exhibit strong territorial hierarchy and aggressive cannibalism, especially during the juvenile stage (5g to 50g).

### Routine Grading Protocol:
- **Frequency:** Every 10 to 14 days for fingerlings/post-fingerlings (1g to 20g); every 21 to 28 days for juveniles (20g to 100g).
- **Grading Screens:** Use 3mm, 5mm, 8mm, and 12mm bar graders.
- **Pre-Grading Conditioning:** Withhold feed for 12 to 18 hours before grading to empty fish guts and reduce ammonia excretion and physical stress.
- **Anti-Stress Addition:** Dissolve non-iodized industrial or sea salt at **3 to 5 ppt (3–5 kg per 1,000 liters)** in sorting bins and recovery tanks to restore osmotic equilibrium and prevent secondary fungal infection (*Saprolegnia*).
